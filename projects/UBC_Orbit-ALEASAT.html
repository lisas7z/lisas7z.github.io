Project Background

UBC Orbit is a satellite design team comprising undergraduate and graduate students from multiple disciplines.

ALEASAT is a 1U CubeSat measuring approximately 10 cm × 10 cm × 10 cm. Its mission is to support disaster monitoring and relief through image transmission from low Earth orbit to amateur-radio ground stations.

The satellite is developed by UBC Orbit and the SFU Satellite Design Team, with support from UBC, the Radio Amateurs of Canada, and the European Space Agency’s Fly Your Satellite! programme .

Mechanical Simulation

Only methodology and basic satellite information are presented here.

Structural Analysis Approach

The analysis evaluates the satellite’s structural response to launch and operational loading. ANSYS Mechanical is used to assess:

  1. Positive margins of safety for structural materials.
  2. Natural frequencies relative to the applicable launch-provider requirements.
  3. Stress and deformation under quasi-static loads in each principal direction.
  4. Response to random vibration representative of the launch environment.

Finite Element Model

The finite element model preserves the important structural and inertial behavior of the satellite while reducing computational complexity. Model simplifications include:

  1. Omitting electronic components with negligible mass contributions.
  2. Representing PCBs with shell elements to capture in-plane and bending deformation.
  3. Representing heavy, relatively rigid components with point masses.
  4. Representing fasteners with simplified connections and specified preload.

The simplified geometry is discretized into finite elements. Bolt preload is estimated using:

$$T = KDP$$
  • T: tightening torque
  • K: torque coefficient
  • D: nominal fastener diameter
  • P: bolt preload

Mesh development includes selecting suitable element types, refining regions near stress concentrations and contact surfaces, and checking convergence as the mesh is refined.

Modal Analysis

Modal analysis determines the satellite’s natural frequencies and mode shapes. These results are compared with the applicable frequency requirements and used to identify modes that contribute significantly to the dynamic response.

Modes with effective mass exceeding 10% of the total mass were identified for particular attention.

Quasi-static Analysis

Quasi-static analysis evaluates stress and deformation under equivalent static launch loads. A 20g acceleration was applied in each principal direction.

Boundary conditions represent the satellite’s support within the deployment launcher. The resulting stresses are compared with material strengths using:

$$ \mathrm{MoS} = \frac{\mathrm{Strength}} {\mathrm{SF}\times\mathrm{Stress}} - 1 $$

The strength limit and safety factor depend on the material and applicable qualification requirements.

Random Vibration Analysis

Random vibration analysis evaluates the satellite’s response to stochastic launch excitation. It supports assessment of structural stresses, component loading, and displacement.

Modal results provide the basis for the analysis. The qualification excitation profile was selected using Table 2.4-3 of the General Environmental Verification Standard (GEVS) .

Stress and displacement responses were evaluated in each axis using 3σ scaling. Margins of safety and displacement limits were checked against the applicable requirements.
