Project Background

The Piano-Playing Robot is a two-year capstone project developed by a team of four with support from the ENPH Project Lab .

The goal is to provide accessible, live piano music by developing a robotic mechanism that can reproduce different dynamics, articulations, and pitches on a standard upright piano.

The initial design focused on one robotic finger capable of playing dynamics from pp (very soft) to ff (very loud), with different note lengths. The broader system includes lateral motion between keys and audio calibration.

Actuator Design

The actuator converts a commanded motion profile into finger movement. Our design uses a rotary voice coil with a current-carrying coil and permanent magnets.

A conductor carrying current through a magnetic field experiences a Lorentz force. For the simplified arrangement shown below, the force magnitude is:

$$F = nBI\ell$$
  • n: number of coil turns
  • B: magnetic field strength
  • I: current
  • ℓ: active conductor length

The force direction follows the right-hand rule and depends on the current direction and local magnetic field. Reversing the current reverses the electromagnetic force.

This reference arrangement informed our rotary voice-coil design for pressing and releasing piano keys.

The finger and coil form one moving body connected to the base by two spring-steel flexure bearings. Permanent magnets are mounted on the base. Current through the coil produces the electromagnetic force that moves the finger; when the current is removed, the flexures provide a restoring force that returns the finger toward its original position.

Audio Calibration

Audio calibration connects the commanded finger motion with the resulting piano sound, allowing note dynamics and timing to be evaluated.
