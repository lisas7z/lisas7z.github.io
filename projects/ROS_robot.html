Project Background

For UBC Engineering Physics ENPH 353 , our team of two built an autonomous robot for a four-minute competition in Gazebo. It had to navigate roads, grass, and mountain terrain, avoid obstacles, read eight clueboards, and obey traffic rules.

High-Level Summary

The robot combined image-based navigation, obstacle detection, clueboard processing, CNN character recognition, and a modular ROS system. Camera data fed the controller and a separate clue detector, allowing sign processing to run alongside driving.

My contributions: I focused on clueboard detection, character trimming, and integrating recognition with the ROS system. My teammate implemented PID driving, pedestrian detection, and the neural network. We jointly worked on roundabout navigation and training-data generation.

Clueboard Detection and Character Recognition

Our pipeline used HSV color thresholds and contour filters to locate blue clueboards, removed the sky from the mask, corrected the board perspective, and separated its characters. A CNN classified the cropped characters into 36 alphanumeric labels. Synthetic transformations varied blur, shape, and noise in the training images.

The final report records 100% training and validation accuracy on synthetic samples in the last epoch. Clue detection during the competition remained less reliable.

Navigation and Competition Outcome

The robot used thresholded camera images to locate road boundaries and compare their centroids with the image center. Navigation combined terrain-specific masks, PID steering, and motion-based pedestrian detection.

Our team scored 29 points: 24 from the first four clueboards and a five-point tunnel bonus. The fifth board was missed in the grass section, disrupting later clue detection. The robot did not reach the final board before the four-minute limit.

Proposed improvements included more reliable separation of connected characters and a fallback route to the tunnel.
